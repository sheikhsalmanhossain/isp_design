Unpaid bills are rarely about customers refusing to pay — most of the time, they simply forgot the due date. For ISPs in Bangladesh, SMS remains the single most effective channel for closing that gap, far more reliable than email and less intrusive than a phone call. This guide covers how to set up automated SMS billing reminders properly, so overdue payments drop without your staff manually texting customers one by one.

Why SMS still outperforms email for ISP billing in Bangladesh

A large share of ISP subscribers in Bangladesh either don't check email regularly or don't have one tied to an account they actively monitor. Every subscriber, on the other hand, has a phone that receives SMS instantly, regardless of whether they have a data connection at that moment — which matters specifically because an overdue internet bill often means the customer currently has no working internet connection to check email with in the first place. This makes SMS not just more effective but structurally necessary for billing communication in this market.

When to send reminders — the timing that actually reduces late payments

Sending one reminder on the due date itself is the least effective approach — by then, the customer may already be scrambling and the connection may already be flagged for disconnection. A better-performing sequence looks like:

  • 3 days before due date — a gentle heads-up reminder, giving the customer time to plan the payment.
  • On the due date — a direct reminder that today is the deadline.
  • 1–2 days after, if unpaid — a firmer notice, ideally mentioning the connection will be paused if payment isn't received shortly.
  • Immediately on disconnection — confirming the service is now paused and exactly how to restore it (usually a direct bKash/Nagad payment link).
  • Immediately on payment — a confirmation SMS that the connection has been restored, closing the loop.

This staged approach reduces the number of customers who miss payment simply due to a forgotten deadline, while the post-disconnection message reduces support call volume since customers already know exactly what happened and what to do next.

Setting up bulk SMS from your ISP billing software

Rather than manually composing and sending texts, ISP billing platforms typically integrate with a local SMS gateway provider to send these automatically, triggered directly by billing events. The general setup looks like:

  1. Connect an SMS gateway account — most Bangladesh ISP software integrates with local bulk SMS providers via API, using an API key rather than manual sending.
  2. Define trigger events — due-date-approaching, due-date-today, overdue, disconnected, and payment-confirmed are the core events worth automating first.
  3. Set up message templates per event — each trigger gets its own message, with dynamic fields (customer name, amount due, due date) filled in automatically per subscriber.
  4. Test with a small subscriber group first — confirm messages send correctly and the dynamic fields populate properly before rolling out to your full subscriber base.

Keep it in Bangla or bilingual where it helps. A short reminder in Bangla (or a Bangla/English mix) tends to get faster action than an English-only message, especially for reminders that need immediate attention like overdue notices.

Message templates that get results

Short, direct, and specific outperforms vague reminders. A few examples of the structure that works well:

Priyo [Name], apnar internet bill ৳[Amount] agami [Date] tarikhe baki ache. Somoy moto payment korun jate connection continue thake. — ISP Dhaka
[Name], apnar package aj expire hocche. bKash/Nagad diye ekhoni pay korun connection active rakhte: [Payment Link]
Dhonnobad [Name]! Apnar payment peyechi. Connection reactive kora hoyeche. Valo internet ba-bohar korun!

Notice each message includes exactly what's needed — the customer's name, the specific amount or action, and (where relevant) a direct payment link — rather than a generic "your bill is due" notice that requires the customer to log in somewhere separately to actually act on it.

Common SMS billing mistakes

  • Sending only one reminder right on the due date, missing the window where a heads-up would have prevented the late payment entirely.
  • Generic messages with no payment link — forcing the customer to separately find how to pay instead of acting immediately from the SMS.
  • Not confirming payment via SMS — leaving customers uncertain whether their payment actually registered, which drives unnecessary support calls.
  • Sending too many messages — over-notifying (e.g. daily reminders) trains customers to ignore the messages entirely, defeating the purpose.
