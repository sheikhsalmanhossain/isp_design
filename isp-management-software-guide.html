If you're running an ISP in Bangladesh with anywhere from 50 to a few thousand subscribers, at some point a spreadsheet and a WhatsApp group stop being enough. This guide walks through what actually matters when evaluating ISP management software — not a marketing feature list, but the practical things that determine whether a platform will hold up as your subscriber base grows.

Why small ISPs outgrow spreadsheets fast

Most ISPs in Bangladesh start the same way: a spreadsheet for customers, a notebook or phone for payment tracking, and manual MikroTik configuration for every new connection. This works fine at 30–50 subscribers. Past that, three things typically start breaking down at once — billing accuracy (who's actually paid this month becomes hard to track reliably), disconnection consistency (overdue customers staying connected because nobody remembered), and staff coordination (multiple people touching the same spreadsheet with no audit trail of who changed what).

ISP management software exists to solve exactly this transition point — not to replace judgment, but to remove the manual bookkeeping that doesn't scale past a certain subscriber count.

Core features every ISP management system needs

Regardless of which platform you're evaluating, these are the features that actually determine day-to-day usability:

  • Subscriber and package management — a single place to see every customer, their package, connection type (PPPoE/Hotspot/Static), and current status.
  • Automated invoicing — bills generate on schedule without manual entry, and overdue accounts are flagged automatically.
  • MikroTik / RouterOS integration — the billing system should be able to talk directly to your network hardware, not just track payments separately from it.
  • Payment gateway support — for Bangladesh specifically, this means bKash and Nagad at minimum, ideally with automatic verification rather than manual reconciliation.
  • Role-based staff access — separating what billing staff, support staff, and admins can each see and change, with an activity log.
  • Reporting — revenue, churn, and collection reports that don't require manually assembling numbers from multiple sources.

Why local Bangladesh features actually matter

A lot of generic, internationally-built ISP billing software technically "works" in Bangladesh but misses details that matter day-to-day for a local operator:

FeatureWhy it matters locally
bKash/Nagad native integrationThe overwhelming majority of Bangladeshi customers pay via mobile financial services, not cards or bank transfer
BTRC report generationA recurring regulatory requirement specific to Bangladesh ISP licensing
SMS billing remindersSMS remains far more reliable than email for reaching subscribers in Bangladesh
Bangla language supportMatters for customer-facing portals and support staff who aren't fully comfortable in English
MikroTik-first designMikroTik RouterOS dominates the Bangladesh ISP hardware market, more than Cisco/Juniper-oriented platforms assume

Beyond MikroTik, most Bangladesh ISPs also run OLT/ONU fiber equipment (commonly VSOL or BDCOM) alongside RADIUS authentication for hotspot deployments — worth confirming your billing software can monitor OLT status and manage ONU provisioning, not just PPPoE secrets, if fiber is part of your network.

Generic global platforms often treat these as optional add-ons or don't support them at all, which means you end up bolting on a separate SMS tool or manually preparing BTRC reports anyway — defeating the point of buying software to remove manual work.

How ISP software pricing works in Bangladesh

Most ISP billing platforms serving the Bangladesh market price by tier rather than strictly by subscriber count, since the actual usage difference between a 200-subscriber ISP and a 500-subscriber ISP is mostly about which features they need (staff accounts, resellers, accounting) rather than raw scale. A typical tier structure looks like:

  • Basic tier — core billing, customer management, MikroTik integration, and payment gateways. Fits ISPs just starting to formalize their operations.
  • Standard tier — adds staff accounts, SMS notifications, BTRC reporting, and bulk import — fits growing ISPs that need multiple people working in the system.
  • Premium tier — adds accounting, reseller management, and often AI-driven features like churn prediction or revenue leakage detection — fits ISPs running full-scale operations.
  • White-label tier — for ISPs that want their own branding on the customer-facing portal rather than the software provider's.

When comparing prices, check specifically whether payment gateway integration (bKash/Nagad/SSLCommerz) is included at every tier or gated behind a higher plan — this varies significantly between providers and materially affects the real cost of running the platform.

A practical evaluation checklist

  • Does it support your exact connection mix (PPPoE, hotspot, static IP) out of the box?
  • Does bKash/Nagad payment verification happen automatically, or does staff still need to manually confirm each payment?
  • Can it generate a BTRC-ready report without manual reformatting?
  • What happens on disconnect — does it cleanly restore the exact prior profile on reconnection, or risk losing custom settings?
  • Is there a real support channel (not just documentation) if something breaks on a live customer network?
  • Can you export your data if you ever need to switch providers, or does the platform lock you in?

Mistakes ISPs make when choosing software

  • Picking based on price alone without checking whether core local features (bKash, BTRC, SMS) are actually included or bolted on separately.
  • Not testing MikroTik integration before committing — this is the feature most likely to have edge cases specific to your router firmware or setup.
  • Underestimating staff training time — a platform that's powerful but confusing for support staff creates its own operational drag.
  • Ignoring migration effort — moving hundreds of existing subscribers into a new system is real work; ask upfront what support the provider gives for this.
